Editorial Overview

SciSpace (formerly Typeset) is a large-scale, highly powerful "AI Research Assistant" platform explicitly designed to accelerate the brutal process of discovering, understanding, and formatting academic literature. While traditional tools either find papers (Google Scholar) *or* summarize them (Scholarcy), SciSpace merges a large-scale internal database of over 200 million academic papers with an proactive, conversational LLM (Large Language Model) overlay, allowing researchers to literally "speak" to the entire global corpus of scientific knowledge. Its primary differentiator is its "Copilot" reading interface and native formatting engine. Firstly, a researcher can upload an incomprehensible physics paper, highlight a complex mathematical formula, and the SciSpace Copilot will literally explain the math formula in simple, 8th-grade language in a sidebar. Secondly, it historically dominated the most painful aspect of publishing: formatting. After writing a paper, instead of spending 40 hours manually tweaking margins and citations to match the specific rules of "The Journal of Cellular Biology," SciSpace utilizes its formatting engine to instantly reformat the entire document with a single click to accurately match the exact guidelines of over 40,000 global journals. It is heavily utilized by non-native English speaking PhD students focused to understand highly complex English academic jargon, exhausted researchers attempting to format their papers for multiple rejected journal submissions without losing their minds, and undergraduate students executing rapid, conversational queries across millions of peer-reviewed papers.

How it works

A medical researcher finds a highly complex, 40-page oncology paper published in Chinese, but they only speak English. They upload the PDF into the SciSpace workspace. They highlight a large-scale block of dense text. In the right sidebar, the SciSpace "Copilot" instantly translates the block. The researcher is confused by a specific term: "Apoptosis pathway activation." They type into the Copilot chat: "Explain this mechanism to me like I am a first-year medical student." The AI scans the specific context of the paper and generates a simple, analogous explanation. Finally, the researcher needs to see if other papers agree. They type: "Does recent literature support these findings?" SciSpace searches its large-scale internal database of 200 million papers, finds 5 recent papers, and provides a summarized consensus citing the new sources.
