Editorial Overview

Read AI (Read.ai) is a highly proactive, deeply analytical meeting intelligence platform designed to passively join video conference calls (Zoom, Teams, Google Meet) and fundamentally change how companies measure the effectiveness of internal communication. While basic AI tools merely generate a transcript and a text summary, Read AI utilizes sophisticated multimodal analysis—listening to audio tone, measuring speaker talk-time, and utilizing computer vision to analyze human facial expressions—to mathematically grade the actual quality and engagement level of the meeting itself. Its primary differentiator is its focus on "Meeting Health" rather than just meeting notes. It functions as an objective, unbiased algorithmic observer. Did the CEO dominate 80% of the talking time in a collaborative brainstorm? Were the engineers visually displaying signs of frustration or boredom (checking their phones, frowning) during a large-scale presentation? Read AI quantifies these inherently psychological, human metrics into a merciless dashboard, actively training organizations to host shorter, more effective meetings. It is heavily utilized by efficiency-obsessed startup founders, remote-first executive teams battling "Zoom Fatigue," and Agile project managers who need to ensure daily stand-ups are actually productive and not secretly causing large-scale organizational disengagement.

How it works

A project manager connects their Google Calendar to Read AI. The manager hosts a 45-minute sprint planning meeting on Zoom with 10 engineers. The Read AI "bot" silently joins the call as a participant. It records everything. 5 minutes after the call ends, the manager receives a comprehensive dashboard. Firstly, it provides standard AI notes (Summary, Action Items). Crucially, it provides a "Meeting Score" of 62/100. The dashboard visually highlights that engagement plummeted at minute 22 when a specific slide was shown. It mathematically proves the manager spoke for 35 minutes, while the engineers only spoke for 10. The system gently flags the meeting as a "monologue" and suggests making the next session 15 minutes shorter to improve engagement.
