Editorial Overview

Trint is a highly specialized, surgically focused AI transcription and audio-editing platform built explicitly for the brutally fast-paced world of elite global journalism, documentary filmmaking, and large-scale corporate media production. While generic transcription tools (like Otter.ai) exist to merely record a basic tech meeting, Trint was conceptually designed by an Emmy-winning war correspondent to fundamentally solve the difficult logistical nightmare of processing hours of chaotic, chaotic on-the-ground interview footage into an actionable, editable narrative story. Its primary differentiator is its "Text-Video Synchronization" Editor. After a journalist uploads a large-scale 3-hour foreign interview, Trint utilizes advanced AI to instantly transcribe and translate the audio. Crucially, the text is glued to the video frame accurately. Trint fundamentally transforms audio editing into text editing. A documentary planner doesn't open Adobe Premiere to edit the film; they highlight a powerful 10-second quote in the text transcript, hit the "Highlight" key, and Trint instantly extracts that specific 10-second video clip. The journalist can stitch together an entire rough-cut narrative video simply by copy-and-pasting sentences in a word document. It is heavily utilized by large-scale global newsrooms (Reuters, Washington Post) breaking large-scale stories on tight deadlines, true-crime documentary editors drowning in 500 hours of chaotic background audio tape, PR teams frantically translating large-scale CEO keynote speeches for global distribution, and qualitative academic researchers processing high volumes of chaotic human interaction.

How it works

A journalist covering a large-scale political rally records a highly chaotic, 2-hour interview with an activist on their iPhone. The deadline is in 3 hours. The journalist uploads the MP4 directly into Trint. Within minutes, the powerful AI transcribed the entire 2 hours with significant accuracy despite the heavy background noise. The journalist navigates the transcript exactly like a Google Doc. They search for the word "Protest." The video jumps exactly to that millisecond. The journalist highlights three devastatingly powerful sentences in the text and clicks "Create Story." Trint autonomously cuts those specific video frames and stitches them together into a 45-second video clip. The journalist instantly exports the rough-cut video directly to the broadcast team, bypassing the entire complex process of scrubbing a timeline in traditional editing software.
