Editorial Overview

Nabla Copilot is an ambient AI assistant designed specifically for clinical environments. Its primary function is to passively listen to doctor-patient consultations and automatically generate comprehensive, structured clinical notes. By acting as a digital scribe, Nabla aims to significantly reduce the administrative burden on healthcare providers, which is a major contributor to physician burnout. Unlike general-purpose dictation software, Nabla operates ambiently, meaning the physician does not need to explicitly dictate commands or summaries. The AI processes the natural conversation flow, identifying medical terminology, symptoms, diagnoses, and treatment plans, and translates these into standard formats like SOAP (Subjective, Objective, Assessment, Plan) notes. This allows the physician to maintain eye contact with the patient rather than staring at a screen. Nabla places a strong emphasis on privacy and compliance, adhering to strict healthcare regulations such as HIPAA and GDPR. Crucially, it does not store audio recordings or generated transcripts long-term, processing data transiently to ensure patient confidentiality while natively integrating into Electronic Health Record (EHR) systems.

How it works

A doctor initiates a patient consultation and starts Nabla Copilot via a web app, desktop app, or mobile device. As the doctor and patient converse naturally, the AI securely captures the audio. Post-consultation, the AI instantly synthesizes the discussion, filtering out small talk and extracting medically relevant information. Within seconds, it generates a structured clinical note, a patient-friendly summary, and any necessary billing codes. The doctor reviews the drafted note, makes any necessary edits or adjustments, and with a single click, exports it directly into the patient's Electronic Health Record (EHR).

Who is it for?

Best For

  • Primary Care Physicians and General Practitioners
  • Specialist clinicians managing high daily patient volumes
  • Telehealth providers and virtual clinics
  • Healthcare organizations focused on reducing physician burnout

Not Ideal For

  • The AI may struggle to accurately capture deeply complex, rapid-fire, or heavily accented conversations where multiple people are speaking simultaneously
  • Requires the physician to verbally state certain objective findings (e.g., "I am noting a slight rash on the left arm") for the AI to include them if they aren't explicitly discussed
  • Direct, seamless integration into older, legacy EHR systems can be technically complex and require bespoke engineering
  • Pricing is generally targeted at organizational licenses, making it a significant expense for an independent solo practitioner
