Editorial Overview

Casetext CoCounsel (recently acquired by Thomson Reuters) is an enterprise-grade AI legal assistant designed specifically for law firms, corporate legal departments, and solo practitioners. Billed as the "world's first AI legal assistant," it leverages heavily customized large language models (specifically GPT-4 architecture) trained exhaustively on Casetext's proprietary, large-scale database of US case law, statutes, and regulations. Unlike general-purpose models like ChatGPT which are prone to legally disastrous "hallucinations" (inventing fake legal precedents), CoCounsel is firmly grounded in verifiable law. Every assertion it makes is directly linked to the specific, validated case or statute it drew from within the Casetext database, ensuring that practicing attorneys can instantly verify its work. It acts as a high-speed paralegal, capable of reviewing mountains of discovery documents, summarizing complex depositions, drafting research memos, and answering hyper-specific legal questions with citeable authority in minutes rather than days.

How it works

Attorneys log into the secure, HIPAA- and SOC2-compliant CoCounsel dashboard. They can select specific "Skills." For instance, they might upload a 500-page opposing counsel brief and select the "Review Documents" skill, querying the AI to "Find every instance where the defendant admitted to altering the financial ledger." Alternatively, they can use the "Legal Research Memo" skill, typing a plain-English question: "What is the statute of limitations for personal injury in California if the plaintiff was an infant?" The AI synthesizes the answer, providing a formatted memo with hyperlinked citations to the exact Casetext case files.

Who is it for?

Best For

  • Law firms ranging from solo practitioners to Am Law 100 firms
  • Corporate in-house legal departments
  • Litigators handling massive document discovery
  • Paralegals and junior associates performing heavy research

Not Ideal For

  • Pricing is exceptionally high, targeting law firms that can absorb the cost through efficiency gains
  • Its knowledge base is primarily focused on US Law, making it less useful for international or hyper-local municipal practices
  • Requires a paradigm shift in how senior partners trust and verify AI-generated work
  • Can occasionally struggle with highly abstract, novel legal theories lacking established precedent
