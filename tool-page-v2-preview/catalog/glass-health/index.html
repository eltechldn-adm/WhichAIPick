Glass Health

Glass Health offers clinical question answering, diagnostic decision support and documentation assistance. Separate clinician, patient and developer offerings serve different healthcare workflows.

Visit Website Compare Glass Health

Editorial Overview

Glass Health is a highly specialized, clinical AI platform built exclusively for doctors, medical residents, and healthcare professionals. While utilizing a large-scale, unconstrained LLM (like standard ChatGPT) for medical diagnosis is intensely dangerous due to "hallucinations" (inventing fake medical studies), Glass Health is strictly bounded, trained, and structurally anchored to peer-reviewed, evidence-based medical consensus and clinical guidelines. Its primary differentiator is its role as a "Clinical Decision Support" and documentation engine. A doctor does not use Glass Health to Google what a rash looks like; they use it to synthesize large-scale amounts of complex patient data. A physician inputs a complex narrative of a patient's symptoms, lab results, and past medical history. The AI operates as a brilliant Chief Resident, instantly generating a heavily cited "Differential Diagnosis" (a list of all possible diseases), recommending the exact next blood tests ordered by standard guidelines, and drafting the grueling clinical note for the electronic health record (EHR). It is heavily utilized by exhausted medical residents working 80-hour weeks who need rapid secondary validation of complex cases, attending physicians drowning in the administrative nightmare of writing clinical "SOAP notes," and medical students building their own highly organized, digital medical brains.

How it works

An Internal Medicine doctor sees a patient presenting with unexplained fatigue, joint pain, and a specific rash. The doctor opens Glass Health. They use "Glass AI" to type the clinical scenario: "35F presenting with malar rash, arthralgia, and fatigue. ANA is positive." The AI instantly outputs a Differential Diagnosis prioritizing Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE), followed by a list of less common autoimmune diseases. Crucially, the AI then provides a "Clinical Plan" recommending specific next steps: "Check Anti-dsDNA and Anti-Sm antibodies," citing the exact rheumatology guidelines. The doctor agrees with the assessment. They click "Draft Note," and the AI instantly writes the full, professional medical chart, which the doctor copies into the hospital's Epic system.
