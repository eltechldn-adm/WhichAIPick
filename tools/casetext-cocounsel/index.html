Thomson Reuters CoCounsel

Casetext CoCounsel (recently acquired by Thomson Reuters) is an enterprise-grade AI legal assistant designed specifically for law firms, corporate legal departments, and solo practitioners. Billed as the "world's first AI legal assistant," it leverages heavily customized large language models (specifically GPT-4 architecture) trained exhaustively on Casetext's proprietary, large-scale database of US case law, statutes, and regulations.

Unlike general-purpose models like ChatGPT which are prone to legally disastrous "hallucinations" (inventing fake legal precedents), CoCounsel is firmly grounded in verifiable law. Every assertion it makes is directly linked to the specific, validated case or statute it drew from within the Casetext database, ensuring that practicing attorneys can instantly verify its work.

It acts as a high-speed paralegal, capable of reviewing mountains of discovery documents, summarizing complex depositions, drafting research memos, and answering hyper-specific legal questions with citeable authority in minutes rather than days.

Best For

  • Law firms ranging from solo practitioners to Am Law 100 firms
  • Corporate in-house legal departments
  • Litigators handling massive document discovery
  • Paralegals and junior associates performing heavy research

How It Works

Attorneys log into the secure, HIPAA- and SOC2-compliant CoCounsel dashboard. They can select specific "Skills." For instance, they might upload a 500-page opposing counsel brief and select the "Review Documents" skill, querying the AI to "Find every instance where the defendant admitted to altering the financial ledger." Alternatively, they can use the "Legal Research Memo" skill, typing a plain-English question: "What is the statute of limitations for personal injury in California if the plaintiff was an infant?" The AI synthesizes the answer, providing a formatted memo with hyperlinked citations to the exact Casetext case files.

Key Features

Legal Core Skills

  • Legal Research Memo generation
  • Contract policy compliance checking
  • Deposition summarization
  • Database-grounded Q&A

Security & Verification

  • Zero-retention data policies (OpenAI does not train on client data)
  • Direct hyperlink citations to verifiable case law
  • HIPAA, SOC2, and GDPR compliance
  • large-scale document upload capacity

Strengths & Limitations

Strengths

  • The established elimination of "generated" cases makes it one of the only LLM tools safe for actual legal practice
  • Data privacy standards are rigorous enough to satisfy the strictest corporate compliance officers
  • Drastically reduces the billable hours required for tedious document review and deposition summarizing
  • The integration with Casetext's underlying search engine provides unrivaled accuracy in US Law

Limitations

  • Pricing is exceptionally high, targeting law firms that can absorb the cost through efficiency gains
  • Its knowledge base is primarily focused on US Law, making it less useful for international or hyper-local municipal practices
  • Requires a paradigm shift in how senior partners trust and verify AI-generated work
  • Can occasionally struggle with highly abstract, novel legal theories lacking established precedent

Pricing

CoCounsel operates on a premium, enterprise-tier subscription model priced per attorney/seat. Due to the high cost of the underlying GPT-4 API and the proprietary legal database, there is no free tier, and trial access is strictly gated through sales demonstrations for verified legal professionals.

How It Compares

Casetext CoCounsel competes at the highest level of legal AI, primarily against Harvey AI and Lexis+ AI. Harvey AI is famous for its exclusivity, initially partnering mostly with large-scale global firms (like Allen & Overy) and operating as a highly customized bespoke solution. CoCounsel differentiates itself by being slightly more accessible to mid-size and solo firms, while boasting what many consider the most intuitive, "skill-based" user interface for attorneys transitioning into AI usage.
