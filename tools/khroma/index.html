Khroma

Khroma is a specialized, AI-driven color tool designed specifically for graphic designers, UI/UX professionals, and brand strategists. Unlike standard color wheel generators that mathematically calculate complementary colors (like Adobe Color), Khroma utilizes machine learning to learn an individual designer’s specific aesthetic preferences and autonomously generates significant palettes accurate$2 calibrated to their taste.

Its primary differentiator is its personalized training phase. Before using the tool, a designer must manually select 50 colors they genuinely love from a large-scale, scrolling gradient. The AI uses this bespoke dataset to train a neural network specifically on that designer. The result is a personalized generator that outputs thousands of color combinations, gradients, and typography pairings that the designer will inherently find beautiful, filtering out the harsh neons or muddy browns they subconsciously hate.

It is heavily utilized during the initial ideation phase of brand identity design or website wireframing, serving as an endlessly scrolling mood board of color inspiration that is mathematically guaranteed to align with the designer's specific artistic style.

Best For

  • Graphic Designers and Brand Identity Strategists
  • UI/UX Designers establishing design systems
  • Illustrators seeking unique color palettes
  • Web developers needing rapid, beautiful CSS gradients

How It Works

Upon first login, the user is presented with a grid of thousands of colors. They click their 50 favorites. The screen goes dark for a few moments as the neural network trains a custom model in the browser. Once complete, the user enters the "Discover" feed. They see an significant scroll of UI components (typography on backgrounds, duotone images, gradients) generated using color combinations the AI mathematically predicts they will love. If they see an accurate$2 pairing for a new logo, they click it to instantly copy the Hex codes, RGB values, or CSS output directly to their clipboard for use in Figma or Illustrator.

Key Features

Personalized AI Engine

  • User-specific neural network training (50-color baseline)
  • significant scroll generation of palettes
  • Intelligent aesthetic filtering (Hue, Tint, Vibe)
  • Automated WCAG accessibility contrast checking

Designer Utilities

  • Hex, RGB, and CSS instant copy functionality
  • Multiple view states (Typography, Poster, Gradient, Image)
  • Favorites saving and library organization
  • Image duotone color application preview

Pros & Cons

Pros

  • The personalized training genuinely works; the generated palettes feel uniquely tailored to the user rather than generic, mathematically computed color wheels
  • The "Type" view is highly useful (and practical) for ensuring text is actually legible on a colored background before committing to a design
  • It completely cures the "blank canvas" decision paralysis that designers face at the very beginning of a new branding project
  • The interface is beautiful, minimalist, and deeply intuitive for visual professionals

Cons

  • The initial requirement to select exactly 50 colors can feel tedious and time-consuming for users who just want a fast, immediate color palette
  • Because it trains on your existing preferences, it can sometimes trap you in a "style bubble," continually generating variations of the exact same colors you always use
  • It is purely an ideation tool; it does not integrate directly into Figma or Adobe via a plugin (you must manually copy/paste Hex codes)
  • Lacks the complex, multi-layered color harmony rules (Triadic, Analogous) favored by traditional color theory purists

Pricing

Khroma has historically been offered as a completely free tool for the design community. There are no premium subscription tiers, paywalls, or feature limits, operating primarily as an incredible portfolio piece and utility built by its independent creator.

How It Compares

Khroma competes in the niche color generation space against Coolors and Adobe Color. Adobe Color is highly technical, strict, and based entirely on traditional color theory mathematics. Coolors is the king of large-scale, fast, randomized palette generation via the spacebar. Khroma differentiates itself entirely through its *personalization engine*. It is not mathematically rigid, nor is it completely random. It is an AI trained specifically on *your* brain, making it the most intimate and inspiring color tool available for a designer searching for a bespoke brand aesthetic.
