Glass Health

Glass Health is a highly specialized, clinical AI platform built exclusively for doctors, medical residents, and healthcare professionals. While utilizing a large-scale, unconstrained LLM (like standard ChatGPT) for medical diagnosis is intensely dangerous due to "hallucinations" (inventing fake medical studies), Glass Health is strictly bounded, trained, and structurally anchored to peer-reviewed, evidence-based medical consensus and clinical guidelines.

Its primary differentiator is its role as a "Clinical Decision Support" and documentation engine. A doctor does not use Glass Health to Google what a rash looks like; they use it to synthesize large-scale amounts of complex patient data. A physician inputs a complex narrative of a patient's symptoms, lab results, and past medical history. The AI operates as a brilliant Chief Resident, instantly generating a heavily cited "Differential Diagnosis" (a list of all possible diseases), recommending the exact next blood tests ordered by standard guidelines, and drafting the grueling clinical note for the electronic health record (EHR).

It is heavily utilized by exhausted medical residents working 80-hour weeks who need rapid secondary validation of complex cases, attending physicians drowning in the administrative nightmare of writing clinical "SOAP notes," and medical students building their own highly organized, digital medical brains.

Best For

  • Physicians (MD/DO) and Medical Residents
  • Nurse Practitioners (NP) and Physician Assistants (PA)
  • Medical students studying for board exams or on clinical rotations
  • Healthcare professionals overwhelmed by administrative charting (EHR)

How It Works

An Internal Medicine doctor sees a patient presenting with unexplained fatigue, joint pain, and a specific rash. The doctor opens Glass Health. They use "Glass AI" to type the clinical scenario: "35F presenting with malar rash, arthralgia, and fatigue. ANA is positive." The AI instantly outputs a Differential Diagnosis prioritizing Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE), followed by a list of less common autoimmune diseases. Crucially, the AI then provides a "Clinical Plan" recommending specific next steps: "Check Anti-dsDNA and Anti-Sm antibodies," citing the exact rheumatology guidelines. The doctor agrees with the assessment. They click "Draft Note," and the AI instantly writes the full, professional medical chart, which the doctor copies into the hospital's Epic system.

Key Features

Clinical AI Engine

  • Glass AI (Evidence-based Differential Diagnosis generation)
  • Clinical Plan (Treatment and Lab recommendation generator)
  • Automated EHR note drafting (SOAP notes)
  • Strict anchoring to peer-reviewed medical literature

Knowledge Management

  • Digital medical notebook (Personal Knowledge Base)
  • Bi-directional linking of medical concepts
  • Community-vetted clinical guidelines database
  • HIPAA-compliant data handling architecture

Strengths & Limitations

Strengths

  • It tackles the single biggest cause of physician burnout—electronic medical charting—by drastically reducing the hours spent typing notes after the clinic closes
  • Anchoring the AI strictly to real medical literature significantly mitigates the difficult risk of an LLM hallucinating a fake dosage for a lethal drug
  • It acts as an incredible safety net for physicians dealing with highly complex, multi-system diseases that cross outside their specific specialty
  • The UI is intensely clean, professional, and built explicitly by doctors for the unique workflow of medicine

Limitations

  • It is a clinical support tool, not a doctor; relying on it blindly without human clinical judgment is unethical and poses large-scale malpractice risks
  • While HIPAA compliant in professional tiers, doctors must still be hyper-vigilant not to paste extreme Personally Identifiable Information (PII) like names and Social Security numbers into web browsers
  • It cannot legally prescribe medication or order tests within the hospital system automatically; it purely generates text advice that the human doctor must manually execute
  • Requires the user to actually understand medical terminology; it is highly dangerous if used by a hypochondriac layman trying to diagnose themselves

Pricing

Glass Health offers a Free tier primarily designed for medical students to build their personal digital notebooks. Access to the advanced "Glass AI" differential diagnosis generation and automated clinical note drafting requires a Pro subscription (billed monthly/annually), which is strictly targeted at licensed healthcare professionals actively practicing medicine.

How It Compares

Glass Health operates in a highly specialized field alongside tools like UpToDate, Nabla, and ambient scribes like Nuance DAX. UpToDate is the established standard encyclopedia—you read it, but it doesn't write your notes. Nuance DAX listens to your voice in the room and writes the note autonomously. Glass Health differentiates itself as the ultimate *Clinical Co-Pilot Workspace*. It doesn't just write the note; it actively helps the physician solve the "puzzle" of the diagnosis by generating differentials and linking directly into a personalized knowledge base, making it a cerebral tool for doctors who want an AI sounding board.
